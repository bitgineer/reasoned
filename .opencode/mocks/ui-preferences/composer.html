Recommendation

Ship the stable middle.

Keep the transcript, collapse the model and thinking row into one chip, and hold the composer at two lines. That is the best balance: it keeps all three choices, the confidence rail, and the lesson readable while the keyboard is open and a long note is in progress, and it keeps the model summary in place.

Default combination

Keep transcript · Summary chip · Two lines

The learner is answering a question about the lesson, so the lesson gets to stay. A roughly 100 px strip is enough to keep the last sentence in view while the card keeps the full answer anatomy.

Two lines is the hinge. A fixed 62 px field makes the dock height predictable, so a growing note cannot push the third choice and confidence rail out of the card. The model and thinking controls are session-level decisions, not per-message ones; one chip keeps both names visible without adding a second line or a focus jump.

Runner-up. Keep · hide while typing · expandable is the best long-note combination. The dock loses about 36 px, so the card survives a four-line note. It costs the model and thinking row the moment the field takes focus, which is why it is not the default.

The three choices

Decision Ship Why
Keyboard Keep the transcript The question is about the lesson. Keeping a short strip preserves the reference and still leaves the card the full answer anatomy. Hiding it saves roughly 100 px but turns the session into a chat box; dimming pays the same space cost without making the text easier to read.
Dock content Summary chip Model and thinking are session-level settings. One chip names both, keeps them one tap away, and removes a second visual target from the answer surface. The full row adds no capability; hiding it on focus makes the controls disappear and shifts the layout.
Composer height Two lines Most explanations fit in two lines. A fixed height means the card cannot be resized by the length of the note. One line hides the start of a real answer; expandable is better for long essays but grows the dock by 44 px and cuts the confidence rail exactly when the note is longest.

Where the question card breaks.

These are the combinations that make the card lose content, not just look tighter. Switch each one in the controls above and compare state 03.

Combination What happens to the question card Why
Keep · chip · expandable
state 03		 The card body scrolls; the confidence rail sits below the card's bottom edge. All three choices remain visible, which makes the loss sneakier. The four-line note grows the dock by 44 px. The transcript holds at 92 px and the dock cannot shrink, so the card gives up about 30 px.
Keep · always · expandable
state 03		 Same clip. The full model and thinking row stays visible and competes with Send for the dock's width. The full row is the same height as the chip, so it does not buy the card any pixels. It adds visual weight without changing the failure.
Dim · expandable
state 03		 Same clip, and the transcript behind it is reduced to 32% opacity. Dimming keeps the transcript's space without preserving readable context. The learner pays the layout cost and still cannot check the formula.
Hide transcript · one line
state 02 or 03		 The card is comfortable. The note field shows only the last line, and the lesson is gone. This is not a card failure; it is an answer failure. The card wins the space by removing the two things the learner needs to answer well.
Keep · chip · two lines
the default		 All three choices and the confidence rail fit without scrolling in states 01, 02, and 03. The card keeps a small margin of slack. It is the combination that survives a long note without hiding the lesson or the model summary.

What the mock cannot settle.

The numbers come from this 380 × 880 CSS frame at 1440 px. Android sp scaling, the real IME height, gesture navigation, and the system font scale will change them. The open question for the real build is whether a 92 px transcript strip is enough to preserve context at 200% type, or whether the answer should switch to the hide policy at large font scales. That is a device test, not a CSS decision.
